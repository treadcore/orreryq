An Efficient Trainer: the GPU Five to Eight Times Faster than the CPU

Check-in report CI-069. Paul Richeson, treadcore.org; Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) as the pair. 28 October 2026. Companion records: LAB-REPORT-BRAIN.md section 107 (and 106 E); LEARNING-DESIGN.md VIII and X.

Abstract

LR 106 E found that the behaviour-cloning trainer ran no faster on the GTX 1080 Ti than on the CPU, the card waiting on kernel launches, uploads and a read of the loss every step. Three changes were made: the recording loaded onto the card once and batches gathered there; every head's loss in one masked log-softmax; the loss read once an epoch. At the old settings the held-out agreement is unchanged (drive 65.5%, gun 64.2%), and a settled epoch fell from 4.3 s to 0.815 s; at batch 32,768 it is 0.643 s, against 5.0 s for the same trainer on the CPU. The rule written before the runs passed.

I. Introduction

A. Intent

Do the list LR 106 E wrote, and judge it by the rule written there.

B. The prompt

Paul's message was the list, pasted from the previous answer:

To make it efficient, in order: 1. Much bigger batches. 2. Read the loss once per epoch instead of every step. 3. Load the recording onto the card once. 4. Combine the per-head loss calculations. 5. Let the CPU version use all 8 cores (it used fewer than 2).

III. Methods

LR 107 A. On calber, the recording of LR 106 D (~/bc/rec60.bin), 12 epochs each: BC_DEVICE=cuda and flex, batch 4,096 at 1e-3 and 32,768 at 1e-3, 3e-3 and 1e-2; flex under /usr/bin/time. A settled epoch is the mean of epochs 2 to 12, each ending at its one read of the loss.

V. Discussion

The card's time had been spent waiting, and removing the waits is what made it fast: most of the gain came at the old batch size. The larger batch added a fifth more. The CPU backend got no faster from the same changes and has no setting for its other cores, so the CPU's part in training is several runs at once rather than one run on more cores. For behaviour cloning and for PPO's update step the GPU is now the place to train.
