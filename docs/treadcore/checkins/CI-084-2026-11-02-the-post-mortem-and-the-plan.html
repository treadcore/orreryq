Abstract

A week of automated training (25 October to 2 November) was read a report at a time and written up as a book: the project, the goals rewritten, Paul's directives in plain words, the week, how the line works, what the numbers said, the lessons, a register of 380 open entries, and a plan in seven chapters with 106 references in IEEE form. The plan's first step was built the same day: every distance, time and count of the brain has a range set by the simulator; the wingman's five constants are weights; eight named personas join the roster; three escort gauntlets measure a wingman's station. The scripted wingman reads 29 of 30 covering its leader, 30 of 30 coming back after a shell, and holds eight tiles for 57 to 59% of a keep-station run. The brain's 151 tests and the simulator's 59 pass; no default moved.

V. Discussion

The week's numbers set the plan's order. The search had moved numbers that were constants in all but name; ranges stop that at no cost to play. The wingman asked for exists in the scripted brain and nobody could touch it; five weights fix that today, and the gauntlets say where it stands: it covers and it returns, and it is slow to close because it is a follower, not a formation. That is the design of chapter 11, and the trained wingman's first number to beat. The personas are honest about the terrain: three hills on six dials. The economy is the large thing and is staged behind game versions, because every version starts the evidence anew. What is still Paul's: the three decree candidates, the reward for going forward, the persona names, and whether a person's ping is always a call.
