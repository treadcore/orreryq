Abstract

CI-011 proposed pricing the landfall by the team board's hostile influence. Reading the code first showed two things. Sight is shared by the whole team, so CI-009's boat-or-teammate split was vacuous. And the board clears itself every second, so it cannot know a gun out of sight. Backlog 35 closes without a part. Measured instead: in 76 of 164 landfall deaths (46%), the team had seen the killer 10 to 60 s earlier, near where the boat died, and lost it. Backlog 36 remembers hostile tanks for the landfall, by a weight whose default Paul sets.

Index terms: memory, partial observability, team knowledge, measurement.

III. Methods

In brain_census (fleet.rs), at each 1 Hz sample, every hostile tank in a living trial seat's observation is appended to sightings[owner] with the tick and its position, once a tick. At a landfall death with killer k, the sightings of k whose age is 10 to 60 s are taken. If any lies within DIRECT_RANGE + 4 of the boat's position at death, the death is "seen then, within reach"; else if any exists, "seen then, elsewhere"; else "not seen". Null run, 16 seeds in ranges of 6, 5 and 5; naval.map, 900 s, respawns 3, the fair census.

V. Discussion

The bots forget a hostile tank the second it leaves sight. The duck remembers for 5 s, the board for 1 s. Nearly half the boats that die on landfall die to a gun the team had seen there within the minute. Memory is the cheapest knowledge beyond sight the brain can have, and the first part a death count supports that the earlier landfall parts (landfall_shy, landfall_wait) can read at once.
