Abstract

The improved bot brain of step 12 had sixteen capabilities built, tested and switched off, none promoted, because no census had measured any of them alone at the twelve seeds the roadmap asks for. This session measured all eleven that are off, one at a time, over the fair census: 12 seeds of 900 s on naval.map, every team in the trial seat, 396 matches. It gave the noise a number, the standard error of the paired difference over seeds. No switch was outside two standard errors. Three never acted at all: board, island_site and escort. The team board in particular was drawn once a second and read by no decision. The session then made the board's posture lean the Auto ladder through a blending weight, w_posture. The first match read with it exposed a mis-calibrated posture: Defend in 94% of picks, because a long-tailed threat field summed every hostile tank on the map. After the fix, twelve seeds read +1.21 ± 1.31 a seat; 48 seeds read -0.81 ± 0.65. The switch stays off. The report closes with a review of the brain's architecture, a comparison of ways to tune its 77 weights by mutation, how to playtest faster, and a proposed form for backlog items.

Index terms: game AI, utility AI, influence maps, paired comparison, standard error, statistical power, evolution strategies, SPSA, CMA-ES, deterministic simulation.

B. The fair census (brain_census, fleet.rs)

For seed index n, the world seed is 0x7EADC0DE ^ (n * 0x9E3779B9). Each seed is played three times, once with each team on the trial settings and the other two on the baseline (CENSUS_FAIR, LR 14), so a start that favours one seat favours trial and baseline equally. A seat's score (LR 8, ledger::score, long_game 0.5) is:

score = 10 won + kills - deaths + 2 held_s / seconds + 0.1 built + 0.05 (road + bridge tiles)

A team's score is its seats' mean. A seed's reading is the trial team's mean over its three matches less the baseline teams' mean over their six team-matches.

C. The statistic (new: ledger::mean_se)

The seeds are the samples. A seed's three matches share its starts, so the seed gives one paired difference d_s. Over S seeds:

latex
\bar d = \frac{1}{S}\sum_s d_s, \qquad
SE = \frac{1}{\sqrt S}\sqrt{\frac{1}{S-1}\sum_s (d_s - \bar d)^2}

A difference is outside the noise when |d̄| > 2 SE, about 95% on a normal approximation. A null run (trial = baseline) gives d_s = 0 exactly on every seed: the matches are deterministic, so the three matches of a seed are one match. The spread then comes only from what the trial changes. A change to a rule that acts in fights perturbs the match chaotically, and that perturbation is the noise.

Power. With a seed-to-seed spread σ = SE √S, putting a true difference δ outside 2 SE takes S ≈ (2σ / δ)² seeds. This is the number every backlog item should carry (section VIII).

D. Each switch alone (scripts/switches.sh, make switches)

For each capability off by default: CENSUS_CAPS=<switch>, and CENSUS_BASE_CAPS=board for those that need the board (team_rotate, bridge_jobs, posture), so the baseline differs from the trial by that one bit. Run three at a time; scores do not depend on load. The microseconds a seat-tick do, so they are not compared across this run. The table is written to build/switches/STAMP/table.txt.

To tell "acted and did not help" from "never acted", the census also reports, for the trial team, the seat-seconds spent in each new part's status codes (status.rs), and the meter now counts the posture picks.

E. The posture lean (Rung::pick, Rung::leaned)

Rungs in order: DefendBase, Fortify, Turrets, Hunt, Expand, Guard. For the rungs that are switched on and hold:

latex
\text{score}(r) = (1-w)\left(1 - \tfrac{i_r}{5}\right) + w \cdot \text{lean}(r, \text{posture})

A leaned rung at index i beats an unleaned one at j < i when w / (1 - w) > (i - j) / 5, so it climbs fewer than 5w / (1 - w) rungs. At w = 0.2 that is none; at 0.25, one; at 0.5, all five. Ties go to the higher rung, and a holding DefendBase is never passed. With no posture, or w = 0, the result is the old first-match pick. The test checks this for all 4,096 combinations of six on-bits and six hold-bits.

F. Proving a fix

Following LR 23: a regression test is run on the old code and must fail there. For the posture's Defend rule the old line was put back, the test run (it panicked on its first assertion), and the new line restored, all in the working tree.

G. Splitting a census (CENSUS_SEED0)

The census takes a first seed index, so one long census runs as several processes over disjoint seed ranges, each on its own core. Their per-seed lists are concatenated, and the mean and error are computed over all seeds together.

C. Findings

Numbered A1 onward so later reports can cite them; L1 to L8 are LR 2's.

  • A1. The top of the Commander is still a first-match chain (L1). think_on runs about 30 guarded branches in a fixed order, each ending in return cmds (afloat, hop, revive, dodge, duck, bridge help, fleet afloat, chain hauling, pings, the fort, leaderless, unseen fire, board orders, the cycle, then the goal). The weights tune inside the branches; nothing tunes their order. The ladder's first-match pick now has a blend (Rung::pick); the chain does not.
  • A2. The team board is mostly unread (L5, half answered). It draws influence, threat, the front, territory, defence points and a posture once a second. Only team_rotate reads the points and bridge_jobs its bridge list; posture (this session) now reads the posture. Influence, the front and territory are read by nothing. Every new board field needs a reader and a census, or it is bookkeeping.
  • A3. The posture itself is a first-match ladder (Defend > Economy > Expand > Connect > Fortify on thresholds), where LR 9 designed scores ("when it scores high"). By Paul's rule it should be five scores and a blend. Backlog 21.
  • A4. A full Observation copied per seat per tick (L2, open). world.observe(p) builds each seat's copy, 16 times a tick. Sharing one per team is 12b's last item, and the only one that touches the sim.
  • A5. Global state by thread. with_settings, new_match, board_for and the meter are thread-local or process-global. Replays are the same because bots run in seat order, and the board is rebuilt by the first member due. It holds, and it is why parallel runs must be processes, not threads, in one census. A census in threads would need these passed in.
  • A6. The doctrine runs every tick inside the Commander, and the Commander throws away what it does not keep (keep_inner). That is simple and correct, but the doctrine's work is spent even when a branch above it decides. At 23 to 33 us a seat-tick it is affordable: sixteen seats cost about 0.5 ms a tick, and at 4x (80 ticks a second) about 4% of one core.
  • A7. Parts sit where they cannot act. island_site is consulted only when a tank has no base, which in a fleet never happens. escort needs a far fetch, which a 900 s match has once. A part's trigger should be checked against the census map before its census: the status-code seat-seconds are that check, and now exist for every part.
  • A8. One map. Every census since LR 11 is naval.map. A part promoted there may only be good there (the islands make bridges and boats matter more than on a generated map). The census needs a map list, and a promotion should hold on at least two maps.
  • A9. Documentation coverage. 120 of 337 functions have no doc comment; crew.rs has 10 of 16 bare. BRAIN-MAP.md shows each.
  • A10. Tests by module. crew.rs and hop.rs have no tests in their own files. They are proven through the Commander's tests and the census, which shows them in combination but cannot isolate them.

D. What the structure does well

Every number is a named weight with a doc line, a slider and a place in the ledger. Every new part is behind a bit, so the baseline is always one setting away. The sim is deterministic, so a match is a function of its seed and settings. That makes the paired census possible at all, and a null run reads exactly zero. Logic reads codes, not sentences (LR 22), and no comparison can panic on a NaN (LR 23).

VI. Tuning the Weights: Mutation, Selection, and Noise

A. The tuner as it is (scripts/tune.sh, make tune)

A (1 + λ) evolution strategy. The parent is the default weights. Each generation makes λ = 4 children. Each child multiplies 15 named weights (target selection, odds, exposure, k_long, c_boat, swap_gap) by exp(σ N(0, 1)), with σ = 0.15. Each child is evaluated by the fair census on 2 seeds (6 matches); its fitness is d̄, the trial team less the baseline. A child replaces the parent if its fitness is higher. Capabilities are fixed per run (CAPS).

B. What it can see

Its fitness is d̄ over S = 2 seeds, so its error is σ_seed / √2, which is 0.6 to 3 a seat by section IV.A. Three effects follow:

  1. The parent's fitness is exactly 0. The default against itself is a null run. Any child whose noise draw is positive replaces it, which is about half of them, whatever its weights are worth.
  2. The winner's curse. The best of λ = 4 noisy readings exceeds its true value by about 1.03 standard errors on average, 2 to 3 a seat for weights that act in fights. The new parent's fitness is that inflated figure, and later children are compared against it, so the run stalls on a lucky parent rather than climbing.
  3. The step cannot be seen. σ = 0.15 moves a weight by about 15%. The effects measured so far, whole capabilities on or off, are under 2 a seat. A 15% change in a weight will be smaller. The tuner, as set, is selecting noise.

Recommendation. First make the acceptance honest: noise-aware (1 + λ) with re-evaluation and the 2-SE rule. That is a small change to tune.sh. Then SPSA, because the fair census already measures a paired difference. One step of SPSA is one census with the trial team on θ + cΔ and the baseline teams on θ - cΔ, and its cost does not grow with the number of weights. Racing on top of either spends seeds where they matter. Every promotion is confirmed on seeds the search did not use and on a second map (A8). Backlog 23.

D. How to see a tuner work before spending a night on it

A planted test. Detune one weight on purpose (say w_wounded to 0) and start the tuner from there; it must find its way back toward the default. If it cannot recover a known large effect, its findings on unknown small ones mean nothing.

VII. Faster Playtests

A. Does it run at an accelerated rate?

Yes. Headless, the world and the brains run as fast as the CPU allows. One 900 s match with sixteen bots took 10 to 11 s on one core in this session, about 85 times real time. Three censuses side by side ran 1,080 matches an hour, about 270 match-hours an hour. The window plays at 1x, 2x or 4x for a person watching. Small scenario tests run faster still: the brain's 67 tests, many of them several minutes of game time, finish in about 60 s together.

B. Describe it, then try it

Today a playtest of a behaviour is a Rust test written by hand: place tanks, set a goal, run N seconds, assert. The tests of LR 16 to 24 are all of this form (a_teammate_finishes_a_part_laid_bridge, a_far_fetch_gathers_the_fleet). A small text format would let Paul describe one and have it run headless at 85x:

toml
# scenarios/bridge-handoff.toml
map = "generated:0x7EADC0DE"       # or a .map file
seconds = 240
caps = ["board", "bridge_jobs"]
weights = { bridge_reach = 40 }

[[tank]]  seat = 0  at = [40, 64]  goal = "auto"  material = "full"
[[tank]]  seat = 1  at = [44, 64]  team = 0  goal = "guard"

[[terrain]]  water = { x = [46, 49], y = "all" }
[[board]]    bridge = { by = 1, tiles = [[47, 64], [48, 64], [49, 64]] }

[expect]
by = 240
bridge_laid = [[47, 64], [48, 64], [49, 64]]
status_seen = ["team_bridge"]          # a status code, status.rs

A runner (make scenario S=...) would build the world, run it, check [expect], and print the status codes over time. A census-sized version would run a scenario over many seeds and give its d̄ and SE. Backlog 24.

C. Shorter matches for early behaviours

Parts that act in the first minutes (the duck, the column) can be measured on 300 s matches, three times as many seeds an hour. Parts that pay late (escort, cycle, bridge jobs) need longer ones (goal 5). The census's CENSUS_SECS already allows both, so each backlog item should say which it needs.

VIII. Placing New Items into the Backlog

A. What went wrong with the form so far

Items 1 to 19 are sentences. Several say what to do and not how they would be known done. None says what a measurement would cost: backlog 18 asked for "twelve seeds or more", and this session found that twelve can only see a difference of 1 to 4 a seat. Items that are done stay in the list with "Done" in front, so the open ones are hard to find.

B. The form proposed

Every new item carries six fields:

N. Title. (date; cause: a prompt quoted, or LR/journal/CI finding)
   Goal g, step s. Hypothesis: what should change, and in which number.
   Proof: the test that fails before and passes after, and/or the
   census (switch, map, seconds, seeds from (2 sigma / delta)^2).
   Cost: matches or hours. State: idea | designed | built (off) |
   measured | promoted | done | dropped.

Items keep their numbers forever, since the journal and the changelog cite them. When an item is done, its state changes and a line in the roadmap's goal says where it went; the open list stays short because the roadmap's backlog section shows the open items first and done ones after. Items 20 to 26 below are written this way.

IX. Discussion and Conclusions

  1. The census can now say how sure it is. At twelve seeds it can resolve about a point a seat for parts that act in many fights, and a tenth for parts that act rarely.
  2. A part that never acted cannot be judged by its score. Identical matches, and the status codes' seat-seconds, are the check. Three parts failed it: board, island_site and escort.
  3. Reading the board found the board's own bug at once. Each field drawn and never read is an untested field.
  4. A reading of +1.2 ± 1.3 is not a result. The 48-seed rerun turned it to -0.8 ± 0.7. Confirmation on fresh seeds is part of the method, not an extra.
  5. The tuner as set selects noise. Making it honest is the first tuning step, before any run long enough to promote anything.

Next (one at a time): backlog 21, the posture as scores; then 23, the honest tuner; then 20, island_site where a naval start chooses.
