Abstract

CI-004 found the shell dodge last held at 43% of the trial team's deaths, and that a sixth of ashore dodges are caught by the shell they dodged. This step tested one cause: tanks dropping the dodge on arrival and driving back under the shell. Holding them at the dodge point until the shell lands (dodge_hold 1) costs 1.18 ± 0.55 a seat over 48 seeds and does not change the dodged shell's hit rate (16.7% against 16.6%). The hypothesis is rejected; the weight stays 0.

Index terms: reflexes, causal test, paired census, weights.

III. Methods

CENSUS_DUMP writes the default settings; dodge_hold = 0 changed to 1 is the trial's CENSUS_WEIGHTS. Three runs side by side, CENSUS_SEED0 0, 16 and 32, CENSUS_SEEDS=16, naval.map, 900 s, respawns 3, the fair census, CENSUS_LEDGER=0. The 48 per-seed differences give the mean and its standard error. The meter's dodge counts sum every Commander's dodges in each run.

The part under test is Dodge::holding (lib.rs): with dodge_hold h, a dodge is held while the tank drives to its point, and also while the tick is before began + h * (until - began); at 1, until the shell lands.

V. Discussion

The meter was built to test exactly this, and it did: the dodged shell's hits did not move, so tanks are not caught after arriving. The cost in score is the errand time the hold gives up. What is left for the dodge is when it starts and where it goes, and first whether it should happen at all.

Next: -dodge at 48 seeds (running); then backlog 30.
