Abstract

This is step 1 of six that move treadcore's training from make to orrery's fleet queue.

  • Families is a job of the queue. make families's test runs for 8 candidates a job, side by side on a host's cores, and each family's share is recorded.
  • 42 candidates ran on the fleet in 3 minutes: the defaults, the queue's two of castle_inside 8, and saskatchewan's 39. All hold every floor.
  • gen64/best is the strongest:
    • census +2.97 ± 0.71 over 55 seeds;
    • held out +2.40 ± 0.28 and +2.32 ± 0.28 over 400 seeds each;
    • the duel family from 76.2% to 84.0%.
  • A fault, fixed: a host that dropped off the network failed eleven jobs and stopped the queue. Lanes now wait for a lost host, and requeue its job.

I. Introduction

A. Intent

Begin moving every training and measurement command from treadcore's Makefile into orrery's fleet queue. Families comes first, because 38 candidates were waiting on it.

B. The prompts

We no longer start training from a make command. Remove the training from make, and insert them all into orrery. Make sure all aspects are accomplished, including collecting the data and incorporating the updates into treadcore itself.

  • "Newest first, all 38": families reads every candidate.

III. Methods

  1. scripts/fleet-plan.py:
    • family_candidates(), family_jobs() and families();
    • in plan(), families take up to half of each top-up;
    • collect includes them.
  2. plan.json: "families": {"converge": ["build/saskatchewan/converge/20261026-181011"], "queue": true, "batch": 8}.
  3. The local test: the rendered job command, with only the defaults, run on the Mac against ~/fleet/q-full-1/census-bin.
  4. Reading the results: python3 -I scripts/fleet-plan.py build/queue/full-1 families writes plan/families.json.
  5. orrery's tools/fleet-queue.py:
    • reachable();
    • in lane(), the removed-host and unreachable-host checks;
    • in work(), the requeue on 255.

Tested on a scratch queue {here: "-", gone: nasser} with six 3-second jobs, then with gone removed from its config.

V. Discussion

make families was a gate a person ran by hand on one machine. In the queue it costs three minutes of the fleet, and it reads every candidate as soon as it exists. The families never stopped a candidate here.

What decides now is the census, and Paul. gen64/best is the strongest candidate, and it is what step 5 (promotion) will be built and tried on.

The nasser fault shows the queue must treat a machine's absence as an event, not as a failure. It now does.

Next, step 2: one ledger of every census, saskatchewan's included.
