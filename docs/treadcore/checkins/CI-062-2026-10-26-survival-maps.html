Abstract

Three survival maps were written and read before training: 7 (low and far from home), 7a (low, go home), and 8 (outnumbered; amended first, because the scripted brain lived only 5% of it as drawn). The net learned map 7a (99.7%) but stayed at 0 on map 7. Probes showed the net's drive actions can dodge every shell with exact look-ahead, so the limit was the senses; a shell contact took the dodge from 90 to 93% up to 95.9%. A traced death then showed what was killing it: a strike drone, not the mortar. The net had no drones up and no sense of the air. With the drone head on and an air contact, map 7 went from 0 to 44.3% (scripted 53.3%). One net seat in a real match died half as often (0.82 deaths in 3 minutes, from 1.73). A team of the net read -20.33 +/- 0.27 in the census: level with a random net, so the rule fails, but +3.96 over the last net, with four times the kills.

Index terms: curriculum, sensor design, upper-bound probe, drone defence, transfer.

III. Methods

On the Mac: make train with the courses in turn, from nets/map4.bin and then from each kept net, held-out seeds from 9,000,000; PROBE=oracle and PROBE=sim (map 5's situation built in learn.rs, nine actions played forward in a copy of the world); MATCH=40 (seeds from 8,000,000). On the census machine at 814d601, in a worktree: CENSUS_NET=nets/map7.bin SEEDS=110 SEED0=500000 MAP=all JOBS=24 make census. Deaths, kills and held are summed from the trial team's lines in every g-*.log.

V. Discussion

The step map, the probe and the trace each turned a wall into a result. What a net needs in a match is now partly known: the bubble, then going home, then the dodge. The census cannot yet tell the net from a random one, but it is no longer at the bottom.
