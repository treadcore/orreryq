Abstract

The scripted brain's choices were recorded through the learned tank's own senses and masks (2,490,248 samples from 60 matches) and cloned into the learned tank's net with Burn on calber's GTX 1080 Ti. On held-out matches the clone, nets/bc1.bin, agrees with the scripted brain far above the commonest-label baseline (drive 65.5% against 36.5%, gun 64.2% against 37.9%), and Rust's Net::forward reproduces the trainer's figures exactly. In the census on columbia it plays worse than the net before it, nets/map7.bin: -2.85 +/- 0.44 paired over 110 seeds. Rule part 1 passed; rule part 2 failed. Timed part by part, the GPU is not faster than the CPU for this net: 6.4 ms a step against 5.6 ms, with the card's arithmetic about half a percent in use.

III. Methods

V. Discussion

Rule part 1 passed and part 2 failed, the case the rule named in advance: imitation measured a decision at a time does not carry into a match, where a third of the decisions being someone else's compounds along a route. The next step is Paul's to choose (LR 106 F): PPO from the clone, a look at whether the senses can carry the drive label at all, or DAgger.

The GPU is waiting, not computing: a net of 15,936 multiply-adds a sample leaves the card's time to kernel launches, uploads and a wait for the loss every step. LR 106 E lists the changes in order and writes the rule for the next timing: under 1.0 s a settled CUDA epoch at batch 32,768 with the data on the card, and faster than the CPU on all its cores, or BC stays on the CPU until PPO.
