Abstract

The two training loops' reports are now read by a written procedure: alive, what it decided, whether that holds, what it costs. The school's first eight rounds learned maps 9 and 10 in a round each and kept every passed course at its floor, but open maps slip between their turns (map 8 from 78% to 55%) and the gauntlet, unguarded, swings (G8 from 93% to 32.5%). Two guards are proposed for Paul: a high-water mark for each open map and the gauntlet as a guard. The read of the census machine found the run's candidate of generation 36 (dodge_horizon 1.08, w_in_range 1.4; held out +1.20 +/- 0.30 and +1.34 +/- 0.27), which goes to families and then to Paul. A fleet of Copal machines is designed: workers pull whole censuses from a queue of files on saskatchewan over ssh, join only after an identity check, and the learned tank grows from islands to one evolution over the fleet.

V. Discussion

The school's rule protects what is passed and nothing else, and the reports show the cost of that exactly where it was not looked for: open maps and single decisions. The guards are cheap; whether to change the rule mid-run is Paul's. The run's candidate is its first since dodge_horizon 2, and partly undoes it; families will say whether the scenarios agree. A fleet needs no new infrastructure, only a worker script and the run reading a queue, because the census was already built of whole, independent, machine-labelled readings.
