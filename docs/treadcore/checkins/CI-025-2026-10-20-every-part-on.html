Abstract

Paul changed goal 4's rule: new behaviours land on, with sensible starting numbers, the census guards that the brain still works, and tuning comes after the features. The planned dodge went on (LR 54); then the eleven parts built and still off went on together, -0.85 +/- 0.59 a seat against them off, inside the noise, at about 30% more brain time (LR 55). Three new parts followed, each found by a scenario family and proved by a test that fails without it: the dodge planned against every shell in flight (two shells 76.5% to 100%, four 56.6% to 99.7%; LR 56), the duck foreseen to the gun's ready tick (76.6% to 83.4%), and a shot held when its turn of the hull would carry the tank into a splash, since a tank has no turret (LR 57). The guard for the three: +0.21 +/- 0.68; dodges caught by their own shell ashore 6.1% to 1.6%.

V. Discussion

  1. On by default changes what the census is for. At 48 seeds it resolves about a point a seat; most parts move less. As a guard it answers the question it can: did the brain break.
  2. The hull is the gun. Treating the guns as free while dodging was a turret's habit in a game without turrets.
  3. Three questions, one model. Where to dodge, when to go back, whether to shoot: drive_to answered each, and each family went to or near 100%.
