Abstract

CI-002 found that leaning the Auto ladder by the team's posture costs about a point a seat, whichever way the posture is computed. This step instrumented the brain to show which rungs the lean moves and where a team's time goes, with no change to play. The lean moves tanks from Fortify to Expand (84% of changed picks), and turret time falls 88%. Economy's score feeds back on building: the castle spends material, and spent material reads as Economy. Turning Economy off restores half the turret time but none of the score (+0.13 ± 0.47), so the hypothesis is not confirmed. The larger finding: on naval.map the ladder decides only 5.6% of a team's seat-seconds; the island hop takes 19%, and 46.7% is spent dead. The census as set measures attrition, and the levers worth pulling are survival and the crossings, not the ladder.

Index terms: instrumentation, time budget, utility AI, attrition, experimental design.

A. Census

As in CI-001 III and CI-002 III.A: naval.map, 900 s, respawns 3, the fair census, 48 seeds in three ranges of 16 (CENSUS_SEED0 0, 16, 32) side by side, board on for every team. The three runs:

Sampling is once a second of game time, for each trial seat, after the world steps. A seat whose tank is not alive counts as dead whatever its last code.

C. The causal test (D5)

If Economy's feedback on building caused the point, then removing Economy, which changes only which posture is read, must recover it. The test compares Economy on and off seed by seed, on the same seeds, against the same baseline.

V. Discussion

  1. The mechanism was found; the cost was not. The lean takes tanks off the castle, and Economy's feedback on building is a real design fault. But removing it does not recover the point. Either the point is noise shared by runs on the same seeds, or it comes from the Fortify-to-Expand moves that the Expand posture still makes. Neither can be told apart at 48 seeds.
  2. Measure the budget before tuning a part. The ladder was the object of three steps of work (LR 24 to 26), and it holds 5.6% of the time. A time budget by status code should be the first measurement of any part, and is now one switch away.
  3. The census measures attrition. With half the seat-time dead, parts that pay late are cut off, and every census since LR 11 has been about who dies first. Backlog 29 tests this directly.
  4. On the architecture (CI-001 A1). Over 90% of a team's living time is decided by the chain of branches above the ladder: the hop, rotation, the dodge, the wedge, rallies. Those are where weights, blends and censuses will pay, and where the first-match order of the chain is itself a design question.
