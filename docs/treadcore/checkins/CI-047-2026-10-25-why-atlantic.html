Abstract

In LR 81, atlantic is the map where the board, the posture and team_rotate each count most. LR 81's logs already carry each match's score parts and the trial team's seconds on each rung, so they were read rather than a new run started. There are two mechanisms, not three: on atlantic the board's whole worth is the posture's, which needs it. An Expand second does not buy more ground on atlantic than elsewhere (+0.69 held a 100 s, mid-table). team_rotate takes 48% of atlantic's trial time off the ladder (elsewhere 3% to 22%) and costs 2.22 held. Without the posture, atlantic's trial team builds 20 more things and 4.6 more bridges and holds 1.35 less. No default changes.

III. Methods

scripts/rungs.py build/switches/20261025-131009 -forget -board -posture team_rotate: for each match line block, the trial team's seat-seconds a rung and its held ground, built and bridges less the mean of the other two teams in the same match, averaged a row and a map; then over every row pooled, the least-squares slope of held ground on Expand seconds a map with its standard error. The ladder share is the sum of the four rungs' seconds, -forget against team_rotate.

V. Discussion

A switch that counts on one map leaves a trace in what the trial team spends its time on there. The census's own lines carried the answer without a run. The meter cannot say when in the match the time goes, or what a bridge costs in builders' time. That is the next line to add if atlantic's bridges are to be priced.
