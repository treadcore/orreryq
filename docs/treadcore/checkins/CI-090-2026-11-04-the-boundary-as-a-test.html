Abstract

Five single-decision tests mark the boundary of smart play around pillboxes and attackers: shoot the hostile pillbox and spare the neutral one; take two pillboxes one at a time from outside their rings; answer the tank that shot you before a nearer one that did not; kite an attacker when alone; stand and fight it with friends near. No brain meets them on 50 seeds, the rules included (24, 0, 48, 34, 56%).

I. Intent

Paul, word for word:

we need to setup a goal where it would be smart to attack some pillboxes while avoiding others. like enemy preference , last attacked enemy we would kite and dance, unless we have backup with fleet allies nearby. we should solve it by creating a coded set of tests that should result into that being the boudray of your question.

III. Method

scenario.rs: pillbox_struck (PillboxDamaged on a placed pillbox), first_hit (the first tank a seat hit), pillboxes_standing. Five files. The gauntlet on this Mac, 50 seeds from 9,500,000, the rules, the untrained net, the six milestones, bc2 and logistics (GAUNTLET_NETS); each file played by the rules on 20 seeds for the failures in words (make scenario). 164 brain tests.

V. Discussion

The rules play these decisions about half as well as they should, and the nets worse: the boundary is real and nobody is at it. G22 cannot tell a passive tank beside its friends from an active one as well as the others tell theirs apart (an untrained net 30%), and is read with G21: a brain that passes both has learned when to kite and when to stand.

VI. What is not built

The styles (Duelist, Gladiator) in the gauntlet; the rules taught the five; crucible's arena given a hostile and a neutral pillbox.

VII. Paul's decisions
