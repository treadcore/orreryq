Abstract

CI-003 found 46.7% of the trial team's seat-time spent dead. This step finished and measured the instruments that name what a seat was doing when it went down, with no change to play. Over 16 seeds and 819 deaths, two situations account for 63% of deaths in 7.7% of the time: the shell dodge (43%) and the boat's run for the beach, landfall (20%). Afloat, one dodge in six ends in a kill. Because a tank dodges only when a shell is on it, the count cannot say whether the dodge helps. The causal test (the dodge off, and dodge_hold 1, each at 48 seeds) is running and belongs to the next check-in.

Index terms: attrition, reflexes, instrumentation, survival analysis, experimental design.

C. Reading the tables

A code's share of deaths over its share of time is how much more likely a seat is to die while holding it. A reflex raises this ratio by being where danger is, so the ratio is a lead for a causal test, not a cause.

V. Discussion

  1. Two situations, not ten. The hypothesis named two or three, and two make 63% of deaths. The ladder, rotation and the fleet, the object of most steps so far, are under 3% of deaths each.
  2. The dodge is where danger is. Its ratio of 9.6 is partly that a tank dodges only when a shell is on it. The meter adds that a sixth of ashore dodges are still caught by their own shell, which the clearance (dodge_clear) and the hold (dodge_hold) speak to. The switch off says whether the dodge saves more than it loses.
  3. Afloat, a hit is a kill (567 of 621). Anything that keeps a boat out of range pays more than anything that makes it hard to hit, which points at the landfall (backlog 30) and the crossings.
