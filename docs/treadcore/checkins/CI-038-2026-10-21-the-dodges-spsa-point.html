Abstract

12g's tuning took the strongest lead on record: LR 43's SPSA end point for the dodge (horizon 2.05, margin 1.52, clear 3.73), +0.88 +/- 0.51 over 96 seeds and not promoted. Read again on today's brain over 192 fresh seeds, with a rule set before the run (promote past two errors), it reads +0.25 +/- 0.32, 0.79 errors. The first job of 48 reads +0.88, as LR 43 did; the other three read nothing. The meter shows the point dodging twice as often and hit by the dodged shell three times as often, for no score. The defaults stay; the lead is closed.

Index terms: tuning, SPSA, the dodge, census, promotion rule.

V. Discussion

  1. Chance, or a changed brain. A 1.7-error reading comes about one time in twenty by chance; the brain has also changed since LR
    1. The census cannot say which; neither makes the point worth

    promoting.

  2. More dodging is not better dodging. The point doubles the dodges and triples the hits by the shell dodged; the score is even (whether the extra dodges were of shells that would have missed is not measured). The dodge's numbers sit on a flat, as landfall_memory did (LR 41).
  3. What this says of 12g. The census at 48 seeds cannot see a lead under about a point a seat; a tuner walking on it finds noise. The next tuning wants a measure nearer the part (a family's rate, the meter) or a part whose worth is larger.
