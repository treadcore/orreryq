Abstract

LR 71's census ended at +5.94 +/- 0.38 a seat over 176 seeds, and its section now carries the final numbers. The census's cost was measured: 3.4 CPU-hours and 5 MB a job, with each job given only a third of a core in the guest VM. The census needs cores and nothing else. This check-in prepares it for a dedicated machine. It adds a manual (CENSUS.md), a resume option (RESUME=, checked to replay a job exactly), JOBS defaulting to every core, and CLAUDE.md, so a session on a new clone has the working rules. LR 72 sets out the method behind 71 sections (directed changes tested by paired Monte Carlo censuses, drawn as a loop). It also sets out what a machine opens: profiles of play, their round robin as a payoff matrix that can show rock-paper-scissors, and rotation among them as a meta-strategy (backlog 60, 61).

Index terms: brain census, Monte Carlo, paired difference, compute, resumable runs, payoff matrix, mixed strategy.

III. Methods

The cost. During LR 71, ps gave each census-bin job's CPU time and elapsed time (44:06 of CPU in 2:22:30) and its resident memory (4 to 5 MB). free gave the VM's 8 GB. The CPU-hours are the four jobs' CPU time to the end. The machine estimates scale those hours by assumed cores and per-core speed; they are estimates, marked so.

The final reading. scripts/pool.sh over the four jobs' logs (pooled.txt). The meter's dodge counts were summed over the lines beginning meter: only. Each job's closing summary repeats the totals, which doubled the first count.

The resume check. SEEDS=2 SECS=20 JOBS=2 make census; one job's test result line removed; RESUME= on the directory. The job was replayed, the old log kept as .partial, and the two compared with timing lines excluded: identical.

V. Discussion

  1. The limit was never RAM. A census is cores. The 96 GB and the 64 GB are equally beside the point. The server's 48 threads, if it has them, are what matter.
  2. The method is the loop in LR 72. Directed changes, judged by paired Monte Carlo censuses against a rule written first.
  3. The machine changes which questions can be asked. Seeds were rationed, so most parts were read at 12 to 48. The round robin of profiles is a dozen censuses: a night in the VM, a few hours on the server.
