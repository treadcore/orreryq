Abstract

The fleet's two machines now read the same single-decision tests for navigation and takeover. A scenario's terrain may be a wall, and six gauntlet files put a maze shaped S, 5 or @ between a tank and a pillbox it must take, or a pillbox and a base to take or to build. The rules, on 200 runs each, pass the S 99.5%, the 5 66.5%, the @ 100%, take a pillbox 99.5%, take a defended base 3.5% and build a base 88.0%; their floors are today's success less two errors. Crucible plays the same files' words on the GPU.

I. Intent

Paul: "Specifically the goal is to naviate a simple maze, Like a maze of S shaped and 5 Shaped, and @ (ampersat at-sign) shaped mazes on test gauntlet maps of player vs environment, where we add takeover takss like Pillbox capture, Base capture, Building a base capture. A series of rewards need to be placed into the GPU and CPU such they can simulate the same score, or at least correlate their fitness." And: "Code the implimentation of more tests, coded in Cuda, for GPU and CPU." Then, asked what to do with the work: "Commit and push both."

III. Method

GAUNTLET=1 GAUNTLET_BRAINS=scripted GAUNTLET_RUNS=200 cargo test -p treadcore-brain --release --lib -- --ignored learn::harness::train, on the Mac, seeds 9,500,000 onward as every gauntlet read; the brain's unit tests, cargo test -p treadcore-brain --release --lib.

V. Discussion

The 5 is the hard maze for the rules: its bowl turns back on itself, and a third of the runs end short of the pillbox. A defended base is nearly never taken: the enemy's tank and pillbox together outgun one tank, and G27's floor is zero until the rules learn it. On the card the GPU's nets passed none of the mazes on their first day, and one of them built a base 56 times in 256 after two segments of training on the tasks.

VI. What is not built

The mazes are open fields with walls, not the fleet's maps; the takeover's squad and carrying are the game's own and not tested here.
