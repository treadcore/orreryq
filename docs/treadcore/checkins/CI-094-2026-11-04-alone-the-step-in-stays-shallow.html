Abstract

The rules passed G21 (alone, kite the one that shot you) on 24.5% of its seeds. Read hit by hit, G21 was lost on the direct gun: the kite waited on the mortar's ring, the mortar tied the gun up 12 s, and the step in's window was open a quarter second of each enemy reload. Of three designs, kite_in_alone was kept: with no friend near the target, the step in ends six tiles from it. G21 rises to 35.5% (+8.3 ± 2.6 points paired over 600 seeds), G22 is unchanged, the census reads −1.12 ± 0.92 a seat, inside the noise, and the families hold.

I. Intent

Paul: "its risky, we will recover. i think it indicates faster training." Then: "good, commit when the census holds, then the ring sensor." And: "write a lab report about these redesigns."

III. Method

G21 and G22 for the rules (make scenario, a commander in seat 0 with the dial in its own table), 200 seeds from 9,500,000; G21 again on 400 from 9,700,000, read seed by seed for the paired difference. The rule before the census: not worse than two errors on 48 seeds of every map, the families holding, the other gauntlets the same. Census on this Mac, JOBS=8, the paste shim under bash. The families with SCENARIO_SET="kite_in_alone=6". The gauntlet with GAUNTLET_WEIGHTS, then as built with the default moved. 165 brain tests.

V. Discussion

The kite was right in its idea and wrong in two numbers that the game had moved under it: the mortar's reload and the step in's depth. Split by whether a friend is near, the depth is the boundary Paul asked for.

VI. What is not built
