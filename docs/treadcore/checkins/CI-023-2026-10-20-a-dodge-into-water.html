Abstract

Scenario files grew from three ported tests into a fast instrument, and in four steps they found three things the census could not. First, the mechanism of the landfall memory's gain: without memory a boat circles at the edge of a gun's sight for 39 s and lands beside it, and with any memory over 2 s it lands on the safe coast in 5. Second, a Commander built directly ran the landfall's weights at 0; this is fixed, and dealt play is unchanged to the hundredth. Third, a dodge could choose a point in the water, be refused, and leave the tank under the shell. The fix, measured before and after on the same 48 seeds, gives 7.1% fewer deaths. One claim made on the way was wrong and is withdrawn: that a Commander at sea never prices a beach.

Index terms: scenario testing, partial observability, limit cycles, reflexes, before-and-after measurement.

IV. Results

Circling, 100 seeds: memory 0 holds 0; memory 2 to 60 s holds 100. Ashore at 6 s with memory, 40 s without (seed of the file). Dealt play after the Commander::new fix: LR 46's 16 per-seed differences identical. Shell scenario: 97 of 100, then 1,000 of 1,000.

V. Discussion

  1. A run that cannot fail tests nothing. Three versions of the landfall scenario passed with the memory on and off. Breaking each found a real behaviour: the binding first choice, a drone seeing for its tank, and the circle.
  2. A trace must sample faster than what it watches. A 5 s trace reported the state after a 5 s crossing and produced a wrong claim.
  3. Many seeds find what one seed hides. The dodge's water bug sat under every census since LR 27, averaged into the meter. Three failing seeds in a hundred pointed straight at it.
