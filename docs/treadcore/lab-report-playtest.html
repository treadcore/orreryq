1. What was built

The window had never been seen here. Journal 0002 recorded why: the VM has no GPU, Alpine installs no Vulkan driver, and wgpu will not start without one. Two things changed today.

The driver. mesa-vulkan-swrast, lavapipe: Mesa's Vulkan on the CPU. It was added to copal's full monty (the desktop install for this VM) so the next machine gets it unasked; this one got it by hand.

The stack. With the driver in, every binary built on Bevy, the game and the engine-only treadcore-warm alike, segfaulted before its first frame. gdb put the crash in llvm::AsmPrinter::doFinalization, on a thread lavapipe had created to compile a shader. musl, Alpine's libc, gives a thread 128 KiB of stack unless told otherwise; LLVM's code generator is deeper than that, and lavapipe does not say otherwise. glibc gives 8 MiB and never sees the problem, which is why nobody upstream has. LP_NUM_THREADS does not touch the compile thread. The fix is scripts/bigstack.c: twelve lines whose constructor calls pthread_setattr_default_np with 8 MiB, built by make configure into build/bigstack.so on any musl machine and preloaded by make run and make lan. No root, no Mesa rebuild. With it the spinner spun and the game ran.

2. The first run: solo

treadcore --solo --players 8: one human seat, seven RuleBots, the default seed. Driven by xdotool from a script so every frame is reproducible; the window was fullscreen for the solo frames and a 1200 x 740 floating window for the networked ones.

The start. Seat 0 at the north start, tick 227. Left: the tank's telemetry (position, heading, armor, fuel, shells, the bay by kind, the bubble, the squad) and the AIR table, empty. Right: the NET line and the roster, eight seats, a depot strip. The map: the ring road, the spoke south, the home depot in team colour, the neutral pillboxes with their 8-tile rings in red, the eight nearest forest tiles in green, the fog beyond the tank's 5-tile vision. The controls overlay was still up here; Tab hid it. This frame is of the HUD as it was that morning; the frame it became by evening is the first picture in the gallery (INTERFACE.md, journal 0014).

Three interceptors on station, a recon out. The bubble is the blue ring at 5 tiles; the interceptors sit on it, each with a link line from the tank, labelled I, link quality and battery seconds. The recon went toward the cursor, 8 tiles south, and the disc it sees is the lighter area over the spoke. The amber ring is detection: a strike crossing it is the warning.

A round out, spotted. Right-click 30 tiles down the spoke. The events say round out, spotted, 2.0s: the recon sees the impact point, so the shell lands inside 0.6 tiles rather than 4. The arc is the yellow line from the tank, the impact point the circle at its end, growing as the shell falls. The gun shows reloading; 23 of 24.

Under fire. Tick 664, 33 s in. The tank has driven south to the home depot (resupplying), the swarm from the map edge has hit it once for 15, a neutral pillbox has hit it once for 15, and !! UNDER FIRE !! is on the telemetry. Two interceptors left; one died to a bot's shell splash, which is what the design says a shell does to a ring. Material 5 after the squad harvested beside the start.

Eighty seconds, tank destroyed. The first solo run, zoomed out: the human tank stood at its start and three bots' indirect fire found it by tick 1 615. The roster shows the bots' scores from depots and pillboxes captured; the event log is theirs. The world hash at tick 1 000 is on the NET line even solo.

The simulation held 20 ticks a second throughout on the CPU rasteriser: tick 664 at 33.2 s, tick 1 615 at 80.8 s. The desktop's CPU meter read 86 to 93 % with one window up.

Three peers on this machine, the step 7 way: no LAN broadcast, an introducer only.

The lag on the two seats is what made the headless process the host: the election picks the peer whose worst link is best, and the lagless one measures 30 ms to each seat while the seats measure 60 ms to each other.

Playing as seat 1, tick 60. Three seconds after the window opened. The lobby had registered, been introduced to two peers, probed them, shared rows, and agreed: host host (0000000000000001). The NET line shows rtt 50 ms, 73 packets out, 154 in, 0 dropped, and the prediction line: predict +0 ticks, in flight 0, checked 25, mismatch 0. Sixteen seats in the roster: alice, bot-seat, fourteen bots of which the last four are Defenders. The east start this time.

Driving, predicted. A left-click 8 tiles south; the tank is the predicted one, drawn where the client's own copy of the world has it, two ticks ahead of the last snapshot (predict +2 ticks). 174 checks, 0 mismatches: every snapshot's tank was bit for bit what the client had predicted for that tick. A bot's strike was shot down by alice's interceptor at tick 338 (p11 intercept: KILL is p11's; you lost Recon (Shot) is a pillbox's doing).

Sixteen seats, twenty-five seconds in. The third run. alice at the north start, two interceptors up, under fire from the swarm; four bots already dead to each other; rtt 150 ms as the host measures it (60 ms of simulated link plus up to a snapshot interval), 241 checks, 0 mismatches.

Back in seat 0. The window was killed with SIGKILL at eight seconds, no Leave sent, and started again with the same identity file. Its lobby registered, was told 0000000000000001 is hosting, pinged it, heard back, and joined with no election; the host logged seat 0 'alice' is back ... its seat and sequences given back and welcomed it at tick 561. By this frame, tick 820, the tank the window came back to had been destroyed by the bots: a human tank that stands at its start for thirty seconds does not survive fifteen bots, which is the game as it is, and a finding for the interface (section 5). 4. The multiclient, explained

What happened between the window opening and "playing as seat 1", with the files that do it.

 identity file         introducer                 host           bot seat
 (alice.id)            :47300                     (id 1)         (lagged)
     |                     |                         |               |
 1.  |--- Register ------->|<------ Register --------|<-- Register --|
     |     every second    |   hosting: false        |               |
 2.  |<-- Introduce -------|------- Introduce ------>|-- Introduce ->|

     |    Heartbeat {tick, hash, rtt_ticks} every 500 ms             |
     |--- Command {seq, Move} ---------------------> applied, acked   |
 8.  |    predict: the copy drives the tank now; reconcile on each   |
     |    snapshot; check bit for bit when nothing is in flight      |
  1. Identity. --identity alice.id reads sixteen hex digits, or writes fresh ones the first time (introducer::identity_from_file). A peer id is what a seat belongs to; keeping it in a file is what lets a restarted process be the same player.
  2. Rendezvous. With --no-beacon nothing is shouted on the LAN. The lobby registers with the introducer every second (Registration::poll), giving the LAN address it sees for itself; the introducer notes the address the datagram came from, which on the Internet is the one a home router rewrote. Every second, and at once when someone new appears, the introducer sends everyone in the lobby name an Introduce with everyone else's two addresses. A registration lapses after thirty seconds.
  3. The punch. Every introduced peer is greeted at both addresses every 250 ms (Lobby::punch) until a packet comes back from it; the address it came from is the peer's from then on (heard_from). On loopback both addresses work and the first answer wins; across routers the first packet out is what opens each side's router for the other's packets in.
  4. The matrix. Probe: ten pings a second to every peer for 1.5 s; the median round trip is this peer's row. Share: rows are exchanged until everyone's is in.
  5. The election. election::elect is a pure function of the matrix: Dijkstra from every candidate, out if it cannot reach everyone even by relay, the winner the one whose worst path is shortest, ties by total then by id. Every peer computes the same answer from the same rows and announces it; the lowest id's announcement is final when rows were lost. Here: the lagless headless process, id 1.
  6. The session. The window sends Join every 500 ms with a session number random to this process; the host answers with the Welcome (player 1, the scenario, the current tick, the map as it is now), in Chunks because a map is bigger than a datagram.
  7. Playing. The host steps the world at 20 Hz with every seat's orders sorted by player, sends each seat what it may see every other tick (unreliable, newest wins), the events it may know about (resent until acked), and a heartbeat with the world hash every half second. Orders carry a sequence number and are resent until acked; the host applies each once, in order.
  8. Prediction. The window's own tank is run by the window: a partial copy of the world from the Welcome, hull orders applied when sent, one tick every 50 ms by the same drive_tank the host runs, every snapshot adopted and the ticks ahead re-driven. predict +2 ticks, checked 174, mismatch 0 on the NET line is that.

And the way back: while hosting, the host keeps registering with hosting set. The restarted window registers, is introduced to a hosting peer, pings it instead of greeting it (a host runs no lobby, but answers a Ping from any peer that has a seat), and decides for it with no election the moment it answers. Its Join has a new session number; the host sees the old peer id with a new session, gives the seat back at the tick the match has reached, and starts the seat's sequence numbers over so the first order from the new process is applied. PROTOCOL.md has the packet-level account.

5. Findings

Numbered, as the journal carries them.

  1. The segfault was the libc's, not the driver's. Section 1. The fix is in the build; make configure says libc musl: built build/bigstack.so on a machine that needs it and nothing on one that does not. A glibc machine with lavapipe would never have found it.
  2. The rtt on the HUD was heartbeat age. The first networked frames read 200 to 450 ms on a link with 30 ms each way. Backlog item 5 (journal 0009) had made the figure the host's measurement, the host tick echoed by the client subtracted from the host's current tick; but the client echoed the tick from the host's last heartbeat, half a second old. It now echoes the newest tick it has seen from any snapshot (Client::poll), and the frames read 50 to 150 ms: the 60 ms link plus up to one snapshot interval.
  3. The telemetry and NET panels overlap below about 700 px. Both are anchored to the top; in a tiled half-screen window the NET panel's roster sat on the telemetry. The HUD assumes a wide window. Interface work (INTERFACE.md).
  4. The controls overlay is the first thing a player sees, and it covers the map. It is help, and it is modal in effect. Same.
  5. After a rejoin the event log stamps events tick 0 until the first snapshot arrives, because push_events takes the tick from the view's last observation. Cosmetic; one line in the window's poll.
  6. A seam at the NET panel's left edge in the 1200 x 740 floating window under Xwayland, a one-pixel vertical line the window's full height. winit guessed a scale factor of 1.083 for the X11 window; the panel background's rounding at that scale is the likely cause. Not seen fullscreen under Wayland.
  7. A human who stands still dies in about thirty seconds. Fifteen bots with recon up find a standing tank by indirect fire well inside a minute, three times out of three today. The bots are playing the game as designed; the newcomer's first minute is not. A start grace, a slower bot opening, or a tutorial scenario are the design's to choose; INTERFACE.md section 4 argues for the lobby screen to carry the first minute's instructions.
  8. The harvest order was refused, "no forest there". The script aimed 0 at a tile beside the start that the frame shows as forest; the order's tile was one off. Whether that is the cursor's tile rounding or the script's aim is a thing to check with the mouse in hand.
  9. The shoot's own trap. A mouse-wheel click to zoom switched the compositor's workspace once (Hyprland binds the wheel over its bar), and typing in the terminal while a frame was due dropped the window out of fullscreen. The floating window captured by region was immune to both. Playtesting on the same desktop as the terminal is a thing to plan for.
