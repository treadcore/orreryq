[0:00] TEN GAINS, WORTH NOTHING
For one week in November, a game got better ten times. Ten changes to its brain. Each one tested on hundreds of games. Each one a measured gain. Added together: six and a half points. Then all ten were measured at once, against the game as it had been a week before. The gain was zero.

[0:25] THE SAME CURVE
How can that be? Here is every change the search tried in two weeks. Two thousand six hundred and sixty-seven of them, each placed by how well it scored. And here is the curve you would get if not one of them did anything at all. They are the same curve. So when we kept the best of them, we were keeping the luckiest. And luck, measured again, goes home.

[0:55] SIGNAL AND NOISE
Every measurement is a little signal, wrapped in a great deal of noise. A machine that learns must tell the two apart, or it will teach itself its own good fortune. This is the story of one machine that learns to play a game, and of the borders it built against its own luck.

[1:19] TITLE
This is how a game learns. Not by anyone's opinion of it. By playing itself, many thousands of times, and keeping only what survives.

[1:32] THE WHOLE, IN ONE PICTURE
Begin from far away. There are three territories. Main is the game as people play it: its rules, its brain, its net. Nothing in main is changed by hand. To the right, eight ordinary machines. They take the game as it is now, and play whole games, to measure one change to its brain at a time. Only what is proved better goes back. To the left, three graphics cards. They take the rules of the world, and grow a small neural net inside a fast copy of it. Only a net the game itself credits goes back. Four arrows. Everything that follows is the inside of them.

[2:17] THE BORDERS
Between the territories are borders, and each has its price. From main to the fleet, the crossing is free: to read is to change nothing. From the fleet to the line: a gain that held on seeds the trial never saw, and every gate. From the line to main: a proof. The new game against the old, nine thousand six hundred games. And from the cards to main: the game's own reading of the net. Twice.

[2:50] THE BRAIN'S LOOP
First, the brain's loop. Thirteen arrows. We will take them one at a time.

[2:59] 1. THE BRAIN'S VALUES TO THE SEARCH

[3:17] 2. THE SEARCH TO THE FIRST READ
The search makes a guess. One dial moved. One switch flipped. Only one, when it can, so that if the game gets better, we know why. A trial is a question, put plainly: is the game better with this one change?

[3:36] 3. THE FIRST READ TO READ ON
The answer costs games. One hundred and fifty of them, on thirteen maps, each played twice on the same roll of the dice: once with the change, once without. Most guesses do nothing. The first read exists to let them go cheaply.

[3:55] 4. READ ON TO HELD-OUT READS
A guess that looks good is read again, with more games and a higher bar each time. But notice the trap. We chose it because it scored high, and part of any high score is luck. So it is sent where its luck must start over.

[4:16] 5. HELD-OUT READS TO THE GATES
Fresh seeds. Games no trial has ever been measured on. If the gain is real, it is still there. If it was luck, it is gone. Up to six such reads are pooled. What survives is a candidate: something we now have reason to believe.

[4:37] 6. THE GATES TO THE LINE
Belief is not enough, because points do not show everything. So the gates ask three things. Is the gain worth a generation? Does every scenario the game must pass still pass? And if it gives something up, does it gain enough to pay for it? Then it becomes one commit, on the line.

[5:01] 7. THE LINE TO THE PROOF

[5:19] 8. THE PROOF TO THE BRAIN'S VALUES

[5:37] 9. THE PROOF TO ORPHANED

[5:56] 10. ORPHANED TO THE SEARCH

[6:11] 11. THE PROOF TO A FLAG
