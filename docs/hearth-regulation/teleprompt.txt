[4:34] ONE BALANCE
The room is treated as one lump of heat. C times the room's slope equals the fleet's watts, plus every other watt, less what the walls lose. The room and the weather are read. So are the fleet's watts, with a model where a machine has no counter. C is measured: four point two megajoules a degree. U A, the watts lost for each degree over the weather, is not measured. One hundred and twenty is the figure in use. P nought, every watt that is not the fleet's, is not measured either. It is what is left over.

[5:16] THREE THINGS FOLLOW
Three things follow. Where the room settles: the weather, plus all the watts over U A. How fast: C over U A. Nine point seven hours, if U A is one hundred and twenty. And the three flows on the Diagram. Through the walls: U A times the gap to the weather. Soak: C times the slope. Other heat: walls plus soak, less the fleet. It is as wrong as U A is.

[5:48] THE BUDGET
The controller does not use gains. It asks how many watts must be added or taken away for the room to stop where it is wanted. The first term is what the walls will lose once the room is at the target. The second is what the room is storing as it moves now. It is held within six hundred watts. Then one step. Graphics machines are kept, and the machine that spends most energy on a job goes first. Forty minutes between changes, because the room answers about an hour late.
